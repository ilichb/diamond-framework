### Description (HTML, four paragraphs)

    <p>Proof-of-stake secures admission and deterrence with a single instrument,
    financial capital, and that is the source of its structural tension: high
    collateral requirements deter honest but capital-constrained validators, low
    requirements expose the network to Sybil attacks. The Diamond Framework
    replaces a fraction of the financial stake with verifiable, non-transferable
    reputation, so the admission barrier falls without claiming that the full
    Cost of Attack is preserved; that question is left open in the paper.</p>

    <p>This deposit is the frozen PDF snapshot of the manuscript, v0.2.3, with its
    source. v0.2.3 is an errata release and changes no contract and no test. The
    paper had printed 286,144 gas as the deployed verification cost; that figure
    belongs to V3's three-input verifying key, while the key the registry installs
    has four public inputs and measures 306,253 gas cold and 247,211 warm. The
    installed key's figure is now the deployed one (break-even 730 gwei, 29x margin
    at 25 gwei, the ingest at 2.2x the verification), and 286,144 is kept only
    where the text says it is V3's: measured on 2026-10-20 at commit 70f225c, with
    a three-input key the registry can no longer install. The full admission path
    is measured for the first time: manager.admit costs 395,255 gas cold for a
    first admission under the installed key (break-even 566 gwei). No conclusion
    of the paper moves: verification stays more than two orders of magnitude below
    the ingest and Bulletproofs stays roughly an order of magnitude above it.</p>

    <p>Artifact: diamond-framework-2026-10-29.pdf, 43 pages, 601340 bytes,
    SHA-256 dd071071bf3b300d50b1355d3c094a84dcdd8ad708dfbf3d7b58a6a748d313d9;
    source main.tex, 158063 bytes, SHA-256
    21efaaf021407fe7ac513d96e8e83c1fc5682e6c3d3105ea265d0a1d0f97812c. The claim

    An OpenTimestamps receipt over that root was stamped on 2026-10-30 (four
    pending attestations, zero Bitcoin confirmations at seal time); the receipt over

    <p>Verification is offline and reproducible: 316 Foundry tests (0 failed, 1
